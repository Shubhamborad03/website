The aquatic adventure

Four gentle stages, paced to your child.

From a parent and baby class at four months, right through to confident young swimmers in squad. Your child has the same teacher and fellow students each week, the way we guide them on their aquatic adventure.

1

Water babies

From 4 months

Gentle parent and baby classes in the warm saltwater. Building comfort, trust and water happiness from the very beginning, with you in the pool alongside them.

2

Toddlers

Little ones finding their feet

Splashing, floating, blowing bubbles and that first big moment of putting a face in. Small steps, lots of warmth, never rushed.

3

School age

Beginners to strokes

From first independent swims to proper freestyle, backstroke and breathing. After school beginner classes run with three children, stroke classes with four.

4

Squad

Confident swimmers, 45 minutes

For swimmers ready to build fitness and refine technique. Squad runs as a longer forty five minute session, with up to six in the group.

How classes work

Tiny groups, warm water, the same friendly face.

Maximum three in the mornings

Morning lessons run with a maximum of three children per instructor. After school, beginner classes have three, stroke classes four, and squad up to six.

Thirty minute lessons

Lessons run for thirty minutes, the right length for little ones to stay calm and focused. Squad runs longer, at forty five minutes.

The same teacher every week

Your child has the same teacher and the same fellow students each week. That is how trust is built and how progress sticks.

Our seasons

When lessons run.

We teach across two seasons each year. Lessons run from January to May, then again from August to mid December, with a rest over winter and over the Christmas break.

Everyone deserves a rest, and we will see you all refreshed for the start of the next season. Bookings are permanent and weekly, so your spot and your teacher are held for you through the season.

"It shouldn't matter how slowly a child learns, as long as we are encouraging them not to stop."
